Faculty of Dentistry, University of Szeged

Dental education was introduced in the University of Szeged in 1960, and the School of Dentistry was transformed into the Faculty of Dentistry in 2007. The English-language dental program was launched in the academic year of 2004/2005. The programme is an undivided master's programme lasting 10 semesters (300 EU Credits), and graduates acquire the title Doctor Medicinae Dentaire.

The programme is designed to unify basic and clinical sciences, proceeding concurrently throughout the curriculum. In the first three years, students are taught the basic sciences of medicine and dentistry. From the second year on, there is more emphasis on the study of dental diseases and their treatment.

The last two years serve as the intensive clinical study of each of the various disciplines of dentistry. In the final year, students participate in elective programmes, clinical conferences, and hospital-based trainings. Furthermore, citizens of the EU will receive their licenses automatically within the Community, and graduates can apply for hospital residency positions worldwide.

Specialized Dental Departments

During your clinical years, you will rotate through various specialized departments. Here is what they focus on:

  • Oral Medicine / Pathology It is primarily a non-surgical specialty consisting of diagnostic biopsies, small excisions, and therapeutic injections.
  • Restorative and Esthetic Dentistry Focuses on restorations, providing harmonious form and function, including cosmetic and digital dentistry.
  • Prosthodontics Utilizes fixed or removable appliances, including dentures, bridges, and implants.
  • Orthodontics and Pediatric Dentistry Also includes the treatment of conditions and diseases related to oral health in children.
  • Periodontology Focuses on the tissues that support the teeth.
  • Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery Handles complex surgical procedures in the facial region.

Dental Degrees Worldwide

USA & Canada: Dentists earn either a D.D.S. (Doctor of Dental Surgery) or a D.M.D. (Doctor of Medicine in Dentistry) degree. These programs are different only in name and signify the same capabilities to practice dentistry. Candidates must first complete a bachelor's degree (usually in biology or chemistry) before applying to a 4-year D.M.D. or D.D.S. program. The first two years focus on classroom learning, while the last two years involve clinical rotations.

UK & Australia: Students earn a B.D.S., B.Ch.D., or B.Dent.S. (Bachelor of Dental Surgery). Later, they can pursue a 1-5 year Master of Dentistry course (M.D.S. or M.Ch.D.). Most UK universities prefer applicants to have at least two years of clinical experience working as a dentist before applying for a postgraduate degree.

Other Nations: In Germany, the title is Dr. med. dent. (Doctor of Dentistry). In Brazil, it is Doutor em Odontologia, and in France, Docteur en Chirurgie Dentaire.
