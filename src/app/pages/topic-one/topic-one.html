Critical Thinking: Self-Diagnosis

In everyday life, we use our hands extensively. From the fingertips and knuckles to the palm (of the hand) and the back of the hand, our hands are versatile tools. We grip objects with our fingers and thumb, feel textures with our fingertips, and use the wrist joint to flex and extend our hands. The nails on our fingertips protect the nailbed beneath. The web between our fingers allows for flexibility and dexterity. Whether we're typing on a keyboard, playing a musical instrument, or shaking someone's hand, our hands play a central role. Through the glenohumeral joint our hand, which is connected to our forearm and humerus is joined to the shoulders, and thus the trunk.

The chest area, the thorax, encompasses the breast bone, collar bone (clavicle), ribcage, and the upper arms. It's here that the ribcage houses vital organs, and the axillae / armpits provide a junction for the upper arms. The crook of the elbow is where we often roll up our sleeves, revealing the intricacies of the ulna, radius, and carpus beneath.

Moving down to the lower body, the calf and thigh of the leg are powerful muscle groups, helping us walk and run. The sole of the foot is where we bear our weight, and the heel of the foot takes the impact with each step we take. The arch of the foot, made up mainly by the metatarsi provides support and flexibility, making it essential for maintaining balance.

In more clinical terms, the tibia, and the thigh bone, or femur, make up the extremities, connected by the knee joint, which is protected by the patella. The pelvic bone / pelvis houses vital organs and provides stability. And if we trace our way up to the back of the head, we find the nape (of the neck), where muscles and nerves come together to control our movements.

When it comes to the face, the frons of the head is a prominent feature. We often touch the bridge of the nose to adjust our glasses. The cheekbones and chin give structure to our face, and the earlobes are often adorned with earrings or earpieces. Just above the Adam's apple, the laryngeal prominence, we can feel the vibrations when we speak or sing. The maxilla forms the upper jaw and holds the upper teeth, while the mandible is the movable lower jaw. The head of the mandible forms the temporomandibular joint (TMJ) with the temporal bone, which allows movements of the jaw during speaking and chewing.

The human body is a remarkable combination of complex structures and everyday functions, from the tip of the nose to the sole of the foot and all the body parts in between.
